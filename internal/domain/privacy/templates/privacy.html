Privacy Policy

Your email stays yours.

Last updated: June 2026  ·  Applies to Grail the app and this website

TL;DR — the short version
  • Grail talks directly to Gmail's API from your machine. Your email never touches our servers — because we don't have any.
  • Your OAuth token is stored locally on your computer only, at ~/.config/grail/.
  • We do not collect, store, sell, or share your data. We can't — we have no backend.
  • This website has no cookies, no analytics, and no trackers of any kind.
  • Grail is fully open source. You can read every line of code that touches your data.
  • You can revoke Grail's access to your Gmail at any time from your Google account settings.
01

Open source — verify it yourself

Grail is fully open source under the MIT license. Every line of code that touches your Gmail data is publicly readable on GitHub. You don't have to take our word for any of this policy — you can audit exactly what the application does.

The relevant parts of the codebase to review are the auth package (OAuth2 token handling) and the Gmail API client (what data is requested and how it's used). Neither sends data anywhere other than directly to Google's API.

02

Data Grail accesses

When you authenticate with Gmail, Grail requests the minimum OAuth2 scopes needed to function. These are the only permissions the application will ever request:

Scope Why it's needed
gmail.readonly Read your inbox, messages, labels, and threads so they can be displayed in the TUI.
gmail.send Send emails when you compose or reply. Grail cannot send email without your explicit action.
gmail.modify Mark messages as read/unread, archive, trash, and apply labels. Only triggered by your keystrokes.
Important

All API calls are made directly from your machine to Google's servers. The data returned — your email content, metadata, contacts — is displayed in your terminal and never transmitted anywhere else.

03

Where data lives

Grail stores two files on your machine and nothing else:

  • ~/.config/grail/credentials.json — your Google Cloud OAuth2 client credentials. This is a file you download yourself from the Google Cloud Console and place here.
  • ~/.config/grail/token.json — the OAuth2 access and refresh token issued after you authenticate. This is what Grail uses to talk to the Gmail API on your behalf.

Both files exist only on your local machine. Grail has no database, no cloud storage, and no remote servers. Email content is fetched from Google on demand and rendered in your terminal — it is never written to disk by Grail.

04

What we don't do

To be explicit:

  • We do not collect your email content, metadata, or any other personal data.
  • We do not store anything on remote servers — there are none.
  • We do not sell, share, or transfer your data to any third party.
  • We do not use your data to train machine learning models.
  • We do not send analytics, crash reports, or telemetry of any kind.
  • We do not display advertising.
  • We do not have accounts, so there is no profile of you anywhere.
Why we can make these claims confidently

Grail is a local CLI application with no backend infrastructure. There is no mechanism by which data could be collected even if we wanted to. The source code is public — if this ever changed, it would be immediately visible.

05

Third parties

The only third party involved when using Grail is Google. When Grail reads or sends email, it makes API calls to Google's Gmail API. Google's handling of that data is governed by Google's Privacy Policy ↗.

No other third-party services, SDKs, or analytics tools are included in Grail.

06

This website

This website (the Grail marketing site you're reading now) does not use cookies, does not run analytics scripts, and does not load any third-party tracking code. It is a static HTML site.

  • No cookies are set.
  • No analytics (Google Analytics, Plausible, Fathom, etc.) are loaded.
  • The only external resource loaded is the Google Fonts stylesheet for typography. Google Fonts may log your IP address when the font files are requested — this is standard browser behavior for any external stylesheet.
  • The contact page uses a mailto: link that opens your local mail client. No data is submitted through this website.
07

Google API limited use disclosure

Google API Services User Data Policy Required disclosure

Grail's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy ↗, including the Limited Use requirements.

In plain terms, this means:

  • Data obtained from Google APIs is used only to provide Grail's core functionality — displaying, composing, and managing your email.
  • We do not use Gmail data to serve advertising.
  • We do not allow humans (including the developer) to read your Gmail data.
  • We do not use or transfer Gmail data for any purpose that is not directly stated in this policy.
  • We do not use Gmail data to develop, improve, or train generalized AI or ML models.

09

Revoking access

You can remove Grail's access to your Gmail account at any time through two methods:

  • Via Google: Visit myaccount.google.com/permissions ↗, find Grail, and click "Remove access". This immediately invalidates the OAuth token.
  • Locally: Delete ~/.config/grail/token.json. This removes the stored token from your machine. You can also delete the entire ~/.config/grail/ directory to remove all Grail data.

After revoking, Grail will no longer be able to access your Gmail until you run grail auth again.

10

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a meaningful way — for example, if Grail ever adds a backend, telemetry, or any form of data collection — it will be clearly communicated in the GitHub changelog and the "Last updated" date above will change.

Because Grail is open source, any such change would also be visible in the commit history before a release ships.
